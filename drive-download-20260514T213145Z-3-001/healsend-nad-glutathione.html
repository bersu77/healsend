The two compounds

Two molecules. Different jobs. Same decline curve.

— Compound One —

NAD+

Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide

NAD+ is the cellular currency of mitochondrial energy production. Every cell — particularly muscle, neurological, and metabolic tissue — depends on adequate NAD+ to convert food into ATP, the energy substrate.

When NAD+ falls, cells produce less energy from the same calories. This shows up as fatigue, slowed recovery, mitochondrial decline, and the subtle dimming of cognitive sharpness that begins in the early-to-mid 40s.

— Compound Two —

Glutathione

The body's master antioxidant

Glutathione neutralizes the oxidative damage your cells accumulate every day from metabolism, stress, environmental exposure, and immune activity. It also drives detoxification — particularly in the liver — and supports skin integrity.

As glutathione declines, oxidative damage compounds. You see this in skin tone, the rate of visible aging, immune resilience, and recovery from anything inflammatory — exercise, illness, alcohol.

  • Faster recovery from inflammation

Asked. Answered.

What's actually in each compound? +

NAD+ is nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — a coenzyme present in every cell. It's not a synthetic or analog; it's the same molecule your body makes (and stops making at the same rate).

Glutathione is a tripeptide composed of glutamate, cysteine, and glycine. Same as the master antioxidant your body produces — directly replenished rather than synthesized internally.

Injection vs nasal — which is better? +

Injection delivers the highest bioavailability — closest to clinical IV protocols at a fraction of the cost. Recommended for fastest restoration and most measurable results.

Nasal uses mucosal absorption, bypassing digestive degradation. Effective and clinically meaningful, more accessible if you're injection-averse. Slightly slower onset.

Many members start with nasal and graduate to injection once comfortable with the protocol. Your clinician will help you decide.

Does oral NAD+ work? +

Limited. Oral NAD+ is largely degraded in digestion, with low and variable absorption. Oral NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR) can raise NAD+ levels but require sustained high doses and are slower-acting.

Direct delivery (injection or intranasal) produces a measurable shift in intracellular NAD+ within days — which oral can't match. This is why we offer those two routes, not oral.

Is this safe? +

NAD+ and glutathione are endogenous compounds — your body already produces and uses them. The safety profile is among the most favorable of any prescribed therapy.

Most reported side effects: mild flushing or warmth during/after injection (transient, resolves within minutes), occasional mild headache. Serious adverse events are rare in clinical literature.

Your clinician reviews your full medical history and current medications during intake and flags any specific considerations before prescribing.

How long should I be on protocol? +

Most members commit to a 3-month initial protocol to fully restore baseline levels and experience the compounding effects. After that, members typically either:

Maintain: continue at reduced frequency to hold the new baseline (most common). Pause: stop and reassess in 6 months — effects typically retain for 4–8 weeks before drift. Stack: add the complementary compound (NAD+ + Glutathione) for full Cellular Reset.

Your clinician helps you build a long-term plan based on your goals and your case.

Is this legal? Is it FDA-approved? +

NAD+ and glutathione are prescribed as compounded medications, prepared by 503A and 503B FDA-inspected pharmacies under prescription from a US-licensed physician. Compounded medications are legally prescribed every day in the US.

These specific formulations are not FDA-approved in the same sense as a branded drug, but they are legally prescribed, manufactured under FDA pharmacy-level oversight, and supported by clinical and preclinical literature.

What about IV NAD+ clinics? +

IV NAD+ therapy at clinics typically costs $400–$1,200 per session and requires 1–2 hours of clinic time. Subcutaneous injection at home delivers similar bioavailability with negligible chair-time and ~70% lower monthly cost.

For most members, the IV route doesn't justify the price or time premium. If you specifically prefer IV protocols, your local longevity clinic remains the right venue.
