Asked. Answered.

What peptide is actually prescribed? +

The Calm Signal protocol prescribes Selank, a synthetic peptide originally developed in Russia and studied in independent clinical literature for anxiolytic effects without sedation or dependency.

Selank works by modulating GABA, serotonin, and BDNF — the neurochemicals involved in stress regulation, mood, and neuroplasticity. Unlike benzodiazepines, it doesn't bind GABA receptors directly, which is why it doesn't produce sedation or tolerance.

We don't lead with the compound name in main copy because we lead with what it does — not what it's called.

How is this different from SSRIs? +

SSRIs work by blocking serotonin reuptake — keeping more serotonin in the synapse. They're effective for many people, but the mechanism produces side effects in a meaningful portion of users: sexual side effects in ~40%, emotional blunting, weight changes, difficulty discontinuing.

Selank works upstream — modulating neurochemical balance through different pathways. It doesn't keep serotonin elevated; it supports the system that produces and regulates it. The clinical effect can be similar; the side-effect profile is markedly different.

SSRIs remain appropriate for many cases. This is an alternative — not a replacement — and the decision is between you and your clinician.

How is it administered? +

Selank is administered as a nasal spray — typically 1–3 sprays per nostril, once daily. Mucosal absorption bypasses digestive degradation, allowing the peptide to reach the central nervous system efficiently without injection.

Most members find the routine fits easily into morning habits — similar to how saline spray is used. Your clinician sets the starting dose and adjusts based on response.

Are there side effects? +

Selank is well-tolerated in clinical literature. Most-reported effects are mild nasal irritation in the first few days, occasional mild headache, and rare reports of vivid dreams.

No serious adverse events documented in published trials. No tolerance, dependency, or withdrawal profile. No sexual side effects. No documented weight changes.

Your clinician reviews your full medical history and current medications during intake and flags any specific considerations before prescribing.

Can I use this to come off SSRIs or benzodiazepines? +

Many members do — and report it as one of the most meaningful outcomes of the protocol. Never stop SSRIs or benzodiazepines abruptly. Coordinate any taper with your prescriber. Your HealSend clinician can also support a structured taper if appropriate.

Selank pairs safely with most existing medications during the transition period. Discontinuation of SSRIs in particular requires a slow taper — your clinician will set the timeline.

Is this legal? Is it FDA-approved? +

Selank is prescribed as a compounded medication, prepared by 503A and 503B FDA-inspected pharmacies under prescription from a US-licensed physician. Compounded medications are legally prescribed every day in the US.

Selank is not FDA-approved in the same sense as a branded drug, but it is legally prescribed, manufactured under FDA pharmacy-level oversight, and supported by published clinical literature primarily from international research.

What if it doesn't work for me? +

Our 60-day satisfaction guarantee covers exactly that. Complete your scheduled clinician check-ins, and if you don't notice meaningful improvement, we refund your most recent month. One-time per member.

Beyond the guarantee, your clinician can adjust dosing or discuss other approaches. Anxiety treatment isn't one-size-fits-all — sometimes the right answer is a different protocol entirely. We don't keep you on something that isn't working.

Is this a substitute for therapy? +

No. The medication addresses the neurochemistry; therapy addresses the patterns. Members who pair Calm Signal with cognitive-behavioral therapy or other evidence-based talk therapy consistently report the strongest outcomes.

If you're not currently in therapy and want a referral, your clinician can recommend approaches and providers in your area.
