What each medication actually does.

01

Oral Finasteride

The most-prescribed hair loss medication in modern medicine. Works by reducing DHT — the hormone responsible for ~95% of male pattern hair loss — at the cellular level.

What you'll notice: shedding slows in 4–8 weeks. Regrowth becomes visible by month 3–6. Maintenance is the goal — keeping what you have, while regrowth fills in.

Foundational. Most members start here.

Low-dose oral minoxidil — clinically a better-tolerated, more potent alternative to topical use. Improves blood flow to follicles, lengthens growth phase, increases density. Works on hair finasteride alone can't reach.

What you'll notice: regrowth in dormant follicles. Increased thickness in existing hair. Best results when stacked with finasteride.

Asked. Answered.

What medications are actually prescribed? +

HealSend prescribes five FDA-approved active ingredients across the three protocols:

Finasteride (oral 1mg) — the most-prescribed hair loss medication globally. Blocks DHT, the hormone responsible for ~95% of male pattern hair loss.

Minoxidil (oral or topical) — improves blood flow to follicles, extends growth phase. Generic equivalent of Rogaine.

Topical Combined Solution — clinician-compounded finasteride + minoxidil in a single daily topical for men preferring lower systemic exposure.

Dutasteride — stronger DHT inhibitor than finasteride. Reserved for advanced stages or rapid progression where finasteride alone won't hold the line.

Ketoconazole shampoo — clinical-strength antifungal that addresses scalp inflammation and supports a healthier follicular environment.

How fast will I see results? +

Months 1–3: shedding slows. This is the protocol working. You may experience temporary "dread shedding" in the first 4–8 weeks — old hairs falling out as new ones push in. Don't panic.

Months 3–6: visible regrowth begins. Density improves at the crown and along the hairline. Photos start to show measurable difference.

Months 6–12: full benefit reached. This is the timeline that matters. Members who stop before month 6 see most of their progress reverse.

What are the side effects of finasteride? +

Finasteride is well-tolerated by ~96% of men. Most-reported side effects are mild and reversible: reduced libido, mild ED, decreased ejaculate volume. These occur in approximately 2–4% of users in clinical studies.

A small subset of men experience persistent symptoms after stopping (Post-Finasteride Syndrome). This is rare and not well understood. Your clinician discusses this risk during intake and can recommend topical-only protocols (lower systemic exposure) if you're concerned.

What's the difference between finasteride and dutasteride? +

Both reduce DHT — the hormone driving most male pattern hair loss. The difference is which enzyme they block.

Finasteride blocks type-II 5-alpha reductase. Reduces DHT by ~70%. Standard first-line treatment.

Dutasteride blocks both type-I and type-II 5-alpha reductase. Reduces DHT by ~90%. Stronger effect, slightly higher side-effect profile.

Dutasteride is reserved for men whose hair loss has progressed past where finasteride alone can hold the line. Your clinician determines eligibility based on your stage and case.

Can I just use minoxidil and skip finasteride? +

You can — but most men plateau within 6–12 months. Minoxidil improves blood flow and extends the hair growth phase, but it doesn't address the underlying cause: DHT.

For men whose hair loss is DHT-driven (the vast majority of male pattern cases), minoxidil alone delays hair loss but rarely reverses it long-term. Combining with a DHT inhibitor (finasteride or dutasteride) is what produces durable regrowth.

What if I have to stop the protocol? +

Honest answer: hair you gained on the protocol will gradually be lost after stopping. DHT returns to baseline, follicles return to their previous trajectory, and within 6–12 months your hair returns to where it would have been without treatment.

This isn't a side effect — it's the nature of how these medications work. They don't "cure" hair loss. They suspend its progression. To maintain results, you stay on the protocol.

Some men plan for this and accept the maintenance. Others use the time on treatment to plan a hair transplant. Your clinician can discuss both strategies.

Is dutasteride safe? +

Dutasteride is FDA-approved for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and has a substantial safety record. For hair loss it's used off-label at lower doses than the BPH indication.

Side-effect profile is similar to finasteride but slightly more pronounced because dutasteride suppresses more DHT. For most men, side effects are mild and reversible. Dutasteride is not first-line. Your clinician confirms it's appropriate for your case before prescribing.

Can women use this? +

Finasteride and dutasteride are not prescribed for women due to risk of birth defects if taken during pregnancy. They're also not approved for female pattern hair loss.

Minoxidil and ketoconazole shampoo can be used by women — HealSend offers a separate consult pathway for female pattern hair loss. Reach out via support and we'll route you.

Is this legal? Is it FDA-approved? +

Finasteride, minoxidil, dutasteride, and ketoconazole are all FDA-approved medications with decades of safety data. They're among the most-studied medications in modern dermatology.

The Topical Combined Solution is a compounded formulation — the active ingredients are FDA-approved, combined in a custom topical by US-licensed 503A pharmacies under prescription. Compounded medications are legally prescribed every day in the US.
